# Candidate civilizations to add

Not-yet-researched civilizations under consideration for the `civilization` category,
ranked by a mix of historical importance and general public recognition. Current
coverage (18 merged topics, plus Mughal researched and pending merge) is listed in
`wikipedia-sources-civilizations.md`; existing regional gaps are sub-Saharan Africa
(only Mali), North America, and Europe outside Rome/Byzantine.

1. **Maurya/Gupta Empire ("Classical India")** — Ashoka is broadly known, golden-age
   Gupta science/math is a strong narrative, but less mainstream Western familiarity
   than the Mughals.
2. **Kingdom of Kush/Nubia** — pairs naturally with the existing Egypt topic, moderate
   public awareness, solid sourcing (pyramids at Meroë help visually).
3. **Celtic civilization** — culturally popular (holidays, folklore, "Celtic" branding
   everywhere) but a looser confederation of tribes rather than a state, which may make
   the event-level narrative harder to source cleanly.
4. **Carolingian Empire** — Charlemagne is a known name, but sits close in time/region
   to the existing Byzantine/Islamic Caliphates topics, so it's a smaller marginal gap.
5. **Aksum/Ethiopian Empire** — historically significant (early Christian state, Queen
   of Sheba legend) but lower general-public recognition.
6. **Srivijaya / Majapahit** — regionally major but not widely known outside Southeast
   Asia; harder sell for general audiences.
7. **Great Zimbabwe** — historically important but thin event-level Wikipedia detail
   (few dated events/figures), makes for a sparse timeline.
8. **Mississippian culture (Cahokia)** — important pre-Columbian North American
   culture, but pre-literate with very few named figures/dated events, hardest to
   source in this Topic/Event format.
